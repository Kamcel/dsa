The Fluid Mosaic Model

Proposed by Singer & Nicolson (1972). The plasma membrane is a two-dimensional fluid of lipids and proteins. Hover over components to identify them.

Interactive Membrane Explorer Live

Phospholipids (~40%)

Amphipathic: hydrophilic phosphate heads + hydrophobic fatty acid tails. Form the matrix.

Proteins (~50%)

Integral (span) vs Peripheral (surface). Transport, signaling, adhesion, enzymatic activity.

Carbohydrates (~10%)

ONLY on extracellular surface as glycoproteins & glycolipids. Cell recognition & ABO blood groups.

Cholesterol

Fluidity buffer: prevents freezing at low T, prevents excess fluidity at high T. Organizes lipid rafts.

Key Concept: Membrane Asymmetry

The outer leaflet is rich in PC, sphingomyelin & glycolipids (saturated, rigid). The inner leaflet contains PE, PS & PI (unsaturated, fluid, negatively charged). PS exposure signals apoptosis to macrophages.

Membrane Fluidity

Three factors govern fluidity: (1) fatty acid saturation, (2) cholesterol, and (3) temperature.

Factor 1: Fatty Acid Saturation

Unsaturated (Kinks)

Double bonds create ~30 degree bends. Prevents tight packing → increases fluidity & permeability.

Saturated (Straight)

No double bonds = straight tails pack tightly. Dense, less fluid, less permeable.

Factor 2 & 3: Cholesterol & Temperature

Mechanism

At low T, cholesterol inserts between phospholipids, preventing crystallization. At high T, its rigid steroid ring restricts phospholipid movement. It also organizes lipid rafts — microdomains enriched in saturated lipids, sphingomyelin, and signaling proteins.

Movement in the Bilayer

Rotational

Phospholipid spins on its axis. Very fast, ~10^7 per second.

Lateral

Movement within one leaflet. ~10^7 per second. Creates fluidity. Proteins also move laterally.

Transverse (Flip-Flop)

Movement between leaflets. Very rare uncatalyzed. Requires flippases (to inner), floppases (to outer), or scramblases (bidirectional, Ca2+-dependent).

Vesicular Transport

Large molecules and particles cross membranes via vesicles — not through protein channels.

Endocytosis vs Exocytosis

Phagocytosis

"Cell eating" — solid particles (bacteria, debris). Forms phagosome, fuses with lysosome.

Pinocytosis

"Cell drinking" — uptake of extracellular fluid and dissolved solutes.

Receptor-Mediated

Highly selective. LDL uptake, iron transferrin. Clathrin-coated pits.

Exocytosis Types

Constitutive: continuous secretion. Regulated: requires signal (neurotransmitters, hormones).

ABC Transporters & Multi-Drug Resistance

Clinical Relevance

ABC transporters like P-glycoprotein (MDR1) are overexpressed in many cancer cells, pumping chemotherapy drugs OUT of the cell. This is a major cause of multi-drug resistance. Inhibiting these transporters is an active area of pharmacological research.

Isolation & Characterization

Understanding membranes requires isolating them intact. Here are the principal techniques from your course notes.

Cell Lysis Methods
Membrane Isolation Techniques

Ionic Interaction

Polylysine-coated plates bind negatively charged cells. Hypotonic lysis leaves membrane attached. Trypsin releases glycoproteins. Chloroform:methanol (2:1) extracts lipids.

Lectin Binding

Concanavalin A (ConA) on magnetic beads binds mannose-rich plasma membrane glycoproteins. Eluted with alpha-methyl mannoside + detergent.

Density Gradient Centrifugation

Sucrose or iodixanol gradients. Light membranes (1.034-1.09 g/ml) vs heavy membranes (1.09-1.136 g/ml). 100,000g for 2.5-3 hours.

Characterization

TLC separates lipid classes. Mass spectrometry identifies species. MALDI-TOF analyzes glycans. HPLC for quantitative lipidomics.

Summary: The Membrane as a Dynamic System

Membranes are not static barriers — they are dynamic, asymmetric, self-sealing, and selectively permeable structures. Their composition varies by cell type and leaflet. Fluidity is tuned by fatty acid saturation, cholesterol, and temperature. Transport ranges from passive diffusion to ATP-driven pumps and vesicular trafficking. Understanding these principles is essential for pharmacology, pathology, and cell engineering.
